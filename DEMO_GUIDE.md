# TĀRA — 60-second TreeHacks application showcase

A polished 60-second demo can be stronger than a long feature list.

**0–10 seconds:** Show the opening title splitting apart and fly through the stars toward Earth. On screen: “You thought you were singing. You were doing math.”

**10–18 seconds:** Show the quick historical story: “No single person invented Carnatic music; Purandara Dasa is associated with organizing beginner exercises.”

**18–30 seconds:** Enter the observatory. Keep the official music stopped to isolate the microphone. Sing a sustained Sa, set your tonic, then sing “Sa Ri Ga · Sa Ri Ga · Sa Ri Ga.”

**30–45 seconds:** Reveal the large pitch graph, click Ga and show the *measured* pitch, `f / Sa`, `1200·log₂(f/Sa)`, `T = 1/f`, `λ ≈ 343/f`. Tap the same note on the graph so the mathematical display changes.

**45–53 seconds:** Show the signal-quality explanation and switch the swara-reference dropdown. This communicates intellectual honesty: swara names depend on tonic and raga.

**53–60 seconds:** Show taala video upload/beat overlay, the famous Carnatic song atlas, and end on “The universe outside us meets the music inside us.”

### Capture checklist

- Use headphones to avoid feedback and screen recording with a separate voice channel, if possible.
- Use a quiet room and a steady, unaccompanied voice; hold each note briefly.
- Keep the M. S. Subbulakshmi tribute player closed whenever recording or calibrating.
- Confirm the web page is the **new build**, not a cached older tab.
- Do not claim automatic raga or hand-gesture recognition: taala beat marking is manual in this release.
- Do not claim the linked song's audio was analyzed: the atlas demonstrates *raga tuning models*, not measurements of the copyrighted recording.
